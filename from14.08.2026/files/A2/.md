## Abstract 

I call this the "classic processing workflow":

$$\text{Expressions} \xrightarrow{\text{Arithmetic Computation}} \text{Scalar Values} \xrightarrow{\text{Comparison}} \text{Relational Structure and "position in the total order"
} (<, >, =)$$

Observing the center: The primary reason we use the real numbers $\mathbb{R}$ today lies in their total ordering and the ability to compare them (<, >, =). Is the intermediate step of "inferring a scalar value" a mathematical ontological necessity for transforming an expression into a relational structure and "position in the total order"? Or does an alternative mathematical space exist that allows extracting such information directly from the structure of the expression, thereby bypassing the step of inferring the scalar value—whether partially or completely?
This is not a proposal to eliminate computation. The goal is to redefine the concept of computation without necessarily relying on intermediate scalar values, thereby yielding benefits.

## 2. Definition


**Requirement A1 - Comparability**

There exists a relation operator $\le_{\mathbb{S}}$, defined directly on the structure of two elements $A, B \in \mathbb{S}$, such that:

$$\le_{\mathbb{S}} (A, B) \iff \text{eval}(A) \le \text{eval}(B)$$

In other words, $\le_{\mathbb{S}}$ is logically equivalent to the standard ordering relations $\{<, >, =\}$ on scalar values—but the left-hand side does not involve the explicit, full computation of $\text{eval}(A)$ and $\text{eval}(B)$.

**Requirement A2 — Structural Distance / Distance Metric**

There exists a function :

$$d_{\mathbb{S}} : \mathbb{S} \times \mathbb{S} \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$$

(or an equivalent ordering structure), defined directly on the expressions, such that $d_{\mathbb{S}}(A, B)$ accurately reflects the difference $\vert{}\text{eval}(A) - \text{eval}(B)\vert{}$ (or a monotonic transformation thereof) without requiring the full computation of $\text{eval}(A)$ and $\text{eval}(B)$.


**Requirement A3 — Total ordering: strongly equivalent to $\mathbb{R}$, but not $\mathbb{R}$**

(Read the definition of COST_ALL below.)

$\mathbb{S}$ must support a total ordering relation with expressive power equivalent to that of $\mathbb{R}$ (sufficient to distinguish between any pair of distinct finite-precision values ​​as required by the problem).
To clarify the constraint of "avoiding issues associated with $\mathbb{R}$": this is not a constraint regarding the nature of the set itself (such as cardinality, countability, or the finite representability of $\mathbb{R}$ on computers—an issue effectively resolved in practice by finite-precision floating-point arithmetic). The actual constraint lies in the processing cost—$\text{COST\_ALL}$ (defined in Section 3): if $\mathbb{S}$ were simply forced to reduce to $\mathbb{R}$ before a comparison could be made, it would revert exactly to the "classical processing workflow" that the problem aims to avoid, rendering the initial objective cost-ineffective—not "set-theoretically impossible," but rather "cost-wise trivial or futile."
If it is simply "isomorphic" to R yet requires a lower COST_ALL, that is impossible.
Therefore, A3 is understood as follows: $\mathbb{S}$ must possess a total order capable of discrimination equivalent to that of $\mathbb{R}$, while the information extraction process must incur a COST_ALL that is genuinely lower than that of the "reduce to $\mathbb{R}$ then compare" approach.





## 3. Two major issues

**Main Issue 1 — General Case**

Given: $N$ expressions, each containing only basic arithmetic operations $\{+, -, \times, \div\}$ with operands being real numbers of finite precision.
Exclusion: All transcendental functions. According to Richardson's Theorem (1968), determining whether $\text{eval}(A) = 0$ holds is undecidable in the general case when expressions are permitted to contain transcendental functions; thus, this exclusion is a necessary condition for the problem to remain decidable, rather than an arbitrary choice by the author.
Open question: Does there exist a system $\mathbb{S}$—along with algorithms satisfying requirements A1–A3 for this class of finite-precision expressions $\{+, -, \times, \div\}$—that achieves a $\text{COST\_ALL}$ level lower than the baseline?

**Main Problem 2 — The Scalar Product Case**

Given: $N$ expressions of a specific (non-arbitrary) form:

$$\langle u, v \rangle = \sum_{i=1}^n u_i v_i$$

where $u_i$ and $v_i$ are real numbers with finite precision.

Open question: With stricter structural constraints (a fixed sum-of-products form rather than an arbitrary expression), are problems A1–A3 easier to solve than Main Problem 1? In other words, does this specific sum-of-products structure allow for a feasible $\mathbb{S}$ that might be difficult to find in the general case?


## 4. Definition of COST_ALL, Classical Baseline


$$\text{COST\_ALL} = \text{Cost}_{\text{computation}} + \text{Cost}_{\text{memory/representation}} + \text{Cost}_{\text{etc}}$$

measured in a real-world environment using specific hardware—not in a computational model that abstracts away resources.

A1-A3 must be feasible within a "realistic environment and specific hardware"—meaning they are subject to hardware constraints or specific computational models, rather than operating within an idealized computational model with infinite resources. The "total actual implementation cost" ($\text{COST\_ALL}$, Section 3) serves as the metric for feasibility—not merely theoretical feasibility (where existence alone suffices), but practical feasibility (where $\text{COST\_ALL}$ demonstrates a genuine reduction compared to the baseline).

## 5. An open problem or an impossible one?

**(i) There is no proof of general impossibility.** No theorem—including Richardson’s Theorem, which merely excludes transcendental functions from the scope of the problem—demonstrates the non-existence of an $\mathbb{S}$ satisfying A1–A3 with a COST_ALL lower than the baseline.
Richardson’s Theorem limits the scope of the problem (excluding transcendental functions to preserve decidability) but does not limit the existence of $\mathbb{S}$ within the remaining scope.

**(ii) There is partial precedent—evidence indicating that this non-trivial problem is not impossible:**
Regarding Main Issue 1: exact or certified geometric predicate evaluation techniques (such as interval arithmetic and adaptive-precision arithmetic—exemplified by the work of Shewchuk and Yap in computational geometry) satisfy requirement A1 (determining the sign or order of an expression) at a lower average cost than fully reducing the expression to an exact scalar value, although these techniques do not necessarily satisfy all three axioms (A1–A3) in every case.
Regarding Issue 2: techniques such as Locality-Sensitive Hashing (LSH), random projection (the Johnson–Lindenstrauss lemma), and Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) achieve A1/A2 approximations for the inner product problem, yielding a $\text{COST\_ALL}$ lower than the baseline across various real-world data scenarios. However, since these are probabilistic or approximate solutions that do not satisfy A3 (which requires an exact total ordering free of error), the question of whether an exact (non-approximate) solution exists remains open.

**Based on points (i)–(ii),** the core problem in Section 4 should be classified as an open problem: there is neither a general solution nor a general counterexample or impossibility theorem, yet there are sufficient partial precedents (in exact geometric computation and in sketching/LSH/MIPS) to indicate that this direction is not trivially impossible. The level of difficulty varies between the two sub-cases: Core Problem 2 (inner product) possesses a more tightly constrained structure and already has many efficient approximation solutions, making an exact solution more feasible; conversely, Core Problem 1 (general arithmetic expressions) involves a space of expressions not constrained by any specific form.



## 6. Avenues of Attack

To satisfy A1–A3, we cannot simply redesign computer algorithms; we must redefine their nature at the level of mathematical ontology. Does the millennia-long reliance on "reducing an expression to a single scalar point on the real number line $\mathbb{R}$" represent a waste of structural information?
The core objective of this section is to construct mappings from the Space of Expressions ($\mathbb{E}$) directly to the Space of Order Relations ($\mathbb{O}$), completely bypassing the scalar space $\mathbb{R}$. The following approaches are based on branches of modern abstract mathematics. **In reality, highly innovative efforts may be required.**

**6.1. Category Theory Approach.** Category theory provides a language to discuss "structure" without regard for "value." Instead of viewing expressions $A$ and $B$ as operations awaiting execution, we treat them as objects within a category of expressions, $\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{E}}$. ​​Arithmetic operations $(+, -, \times, \div)$ serve as morphisms that connect or transform these objects. The approach involves finding a structure-preserving functor $\mathcal{F}$ that maps directly from $\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{O}}$ to a totally ordered category $\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{O}}$ (where morphisms represent $\le$ relations). If such a functor $\mathcal{F}$ exists, comparing $A$ and $B$ reduces to proving the existence of a morphism $f: \mathcal{F}(A) \to \mathcal{F}(B)$ within $\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{O}}$. The mathematics here does not involve performing addition or subtraction, but rather analyzing the commutativity of diagrams (diagram chasing). If a functor can be designed with lower computational complexity (for diagram traversal) than algebraic reduction, then problems A1 and A3 are effectively solved.
**6.2. Algebraic Topology and Directed Homotopy Type Theory.** In the space of expressions, if $\text{eval}(A) = \text{eval}(B)$, then $A$ and $B$ can be considered homotopic—meaning that the structure of $A$ can be continuously deformed (via equivalent algebraic transformations) into $B$ without altering the final "meaning.". Approach: Equality ($=$) corresponds to homotopy; so, what do "greater than" and "less than" ($<, >$) represent? This can be modeled using Directed Topology. We construct a topological space where each expression is a point, and operations generate directed flows. The relation $A < B$ is equivalent to the existence of a directed path from the subspace containing $A$ to the subspace containing $B$.
To determine whether $A < B$, we do not need to traverse the entire path (i.e., we do not need to compute the actual values). We simply need to calculate the topological invariants of $A$ and $B$ (such as homology groups) to ascertain the feasibility of such a path. Topological invariants can often be computed directly and extremely efficiently from the geometric structure of the expressions.
**6.3. Tropical Geometry.** Tropical geometry revolutionized algebra by redefining operations: replacing addition with the minimum or maximum operation, and multiplication with addition (e.g., $x \oplus y = \max(x, y)$). This transforms complex algebraic curves into piecewise-linear polytopal graphs. Approach: We can construct a form of "Extended Tropical Geometry" to map expressions from Problem 1 and Problem 2 onto geometric structures (polytopes) in a high-dimensional space. The distance function $d_{\mathbb{S}}$ (Requirement A2) would then correspond to the Hausdorff distance or Wasserstein distance between these two polyhedra. Comparing two polyhedra in geometry can sometimes be achieved by considering only their boundary vertices or faces—using measure theory—while completely disregarding their "interior" (which corresponds to intermediate calculations). This approach is particularly feasible for Problem 2 (the inner product), where the sum-of-products structure exhibits a high degree of geometric symmetry.
**6.4. Operator Algebras and Hilbert Spaces – For Problem 2 (Inner Product).** Regarding Problem 2, the dot product of two vectors $u$ and $v$ does not necessarily have to be viewed in the algebraic form $\sum u_i v_i$. Attack: We upgrade scalar quantities to linear operators acting on a Hilbert space. The expression $\langle u, v \rangle$ becomes an evaluation of the spectrum or trace of these operators. By employing spectral theory, we can assess the relative magnitude of two operator systems based on eigenvalue bounds, without needing to calculate each individual element precisely.
Toán học lượng tử (Quantum mechanics) đã sử dụng kỹ thuật này hàng thập kỷ: người ta không đo lường chính xác trạng thái vô hướng của một hạt, mà so sánh các toán tử khả quan sát (observables). Mô hình này cung cấp một nền tảng toán học nguyên thủy tuyệt vời để đạt được một COST_ALL thấp hơn trong việc thiết lập thứ tự.
**The pursuit of the space $\mathbb{S}$ is not merely a problem of computer algorithm optimization, but an effort to discover a new "semantics" for arithmetic expressions—one where order arises from the shape of the structure rather than from the result of a calculation.**
