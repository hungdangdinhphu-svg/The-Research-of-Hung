# Comparing and Totally Ordering Mathematical Expressions: Is the Traditional Process Redundant?

Hung Dinh Phu Dang

## Abstract 

I call this the "classic processing workflow":


$$\text{Expressions} \xrightarrow{\text{Arithmetic Computation}} \text{Scalar Values} \xrightarrow{\text{Comparison}} \text{Relational Structure and "Spacing between expressions"
} (<, >, =)$$

Observing the center: In many applications, the final scalar value is not what is truly needed; rather, what matters is the relative ordering or distance between expressions. Here, the scalar value merely serves as an intermediate "carrier" for that relational information.

Is the intermediate step of “deriving a scalar value” a mathematically ontological necessity for transitioning from an expression to an ordering or distance relationship? Or does there exist an alternative mathematical space that allows for the extraction of such a relationship directly from the expression's structure, thereby bypassing the scalar derivation step—either partially or entirely?

This is not a proposal to eliminate computation. The goal is to redefine the concept of computation to encompass structural comparisons that do not necessarily pass through intermediate scalar values.

## 1. Definition

**Axiom A1 — Comparability**

There exists a relational operator $\preceq_{\mathbb{S}}$, defined directly on the structure (graph/syntax tree) of two elements $A, B \in \mathbb{S}$, such that:

$$\preceq_{\mathbb{S}} (A, B) \iff \operatorname{eval}(A) \le \operatorname{eval}(B)$$
